# Hidden Damage

<Box x="0.75" y="1.8" w="4.1" h="2.65" fill="card" accent="top" pad="25px 20px 0 25px" size="10.5" after="4" class="today">

### Today's armor (Kevlar, Dyneema)

- Excellent against bullets, weaker against large impacts
- Repeated hits cause internal micro-cracks
- Damage is often invisible from the outside
- Inspection needs infrared thermography or acoustic emission: large, costly equipment

</Box>

<Box x="5.15" y="1.8" w="4.1" h="2.65" fill="red" pad="25px 20px 0 25px" size="10.5" after="4" bullet="white">

### Self-sensing armor

- The armor itself is the sensor
- Cracks change its electrical resistance
- Can be read with a simple multimeter
- 3D printed, so it can be customized to the wearer

</Box>

<Box x="0.75" y="4.6" w="8.5" head size="14">Can we build armor whose electrical signal reveals how much damage it has absorbed?</Box>

# How Can Plastic Feel?

<Box x="0.75" y="1.62" w="8.5" size="10.5" color="muted">Conductive filament is plastic loaded with carbon-black particles. Touching particles form a network of tiny electrical pathways.</Box>
<Box x="1.75" y="1.97" w="6.5" h="2.48" svg="deck/piezo.svg" alt="Intact carbon pathways give low resistance; cracked pathways make the current detour, so resistance rises" />
<Box x="0.75" y="4.62" w="8.5" h="0.5" fill="red" pad="0 15px 0 20px" anchor="middle" size="10"><span><strong>Like a city's road grid:</strong> close a few streets (cracks) and traffic (current) must detour. Longer detours mean higher resistance.</span></Box>

# Two Materials, Two Jobs

<Box x="0.75" y="1.62" w="8.5" size="10.5" color="muted">Both filaments conduct electricity, but they behave very differently under load.</Box>

<Box x="0.75" y="2.1" w="4.1" h="2.2" fill="red" pad="20px 20px 0 25px" size="11.5" after="4" bullet="white">

<h3 style="font-size:27.8px;margin:0">CB-PLA</h3>
<p style="font-size:13.2px;font-weight:700;margin-bottom:11px">Rigid · carbon-black polylactic acid</p>

- Higher conductivity and stiffness
- Cracks show up as resistance changes
- **Used for the glass sponge lattices**

</Box>

<Box x="5.15" y="2.1" w="4.1" h="2.2" fill="card2" pad="20px 20px 0 25px" size="11.5" after="4">

<h3 style="font-size:27.8px;margin:0;color:var(--red-text)">CB-TPU</h3>
<p style="font-size:13.2px;font-weight:700;margin-bottom:11px;color:var(--muted)">Flexible · carbon-black thermoplastic polyurethane</p>

- Bends and springs back
- Absorbs shock and vibration
- **Used for the mantis shrimp arches**

</Box>

<Box x="0.75" y="4.55" w="8.5" head size="12.5">Rigid layers to sense damage, flexible layers to soak up impact.</Box>

# From CAD to Printed Part

<Glow cx="5.45" cy="4.2" r="0.6" :ring="false" />
<Box x="0.75" y="1.75" w="5.74" h="2.21" img="deck/printed.jpg" alt="Printed grid, glass sponge and mantis shrimp models with dimensions" />

<Box x="6.7" y="1.75" w="2.55" h="2.73" size="9.5" after="3">

#### Print settings

- Bambu Lab A1 and H2C, sliced in Bambu Studio
- 0.4 mm nozzle, 15% gyroid infill
- Horizontal vs. vertical orientation tested

#### Taming flexible TPU

- Kept dry and loaded without the PTFE tube
- Idler screws loosened 1.5 turns, filament hand-fed

#### What went wrong

- Stringing on small features
- 0.75 mm lattice was too fine for the nozzle: only the frame printed

</Box>

# Squeeze and Measure

<Box x="0.75" y="1.74" w="5.1" h="2.72" img="deck/setup.jpg" alt="Compression test setup: force gauge, hand crank, multimeter with alligator clips and the printed lattice" />

<Box x="6.15" y="1.74" w="3.1" h="3.2" size="9.5" after="3">

#### The rig

- Hand-cranked stand with a 500 N force gauge
- 16 mm cylindrical tip presses the sample
- Digital scale tracks displacement
- Multimeter with alligator clips reads resistance

#### The method

- Every model tested 3 times, results averaged
- FreeCAD finite element analysis to predict stress and failure points

</Box>

<Box x="0.75" y="4.62" w="5.1" h="0.42" fill="red" pad="0 10px 0 17px" anchor="middle" size="9.5"><strong>Force and resistance are recorded at the same time as each model is crushed.</strong></Box>

# Lessons Learned

<Box x="0.75" y="1.8" w="4.1" h="2.75" fill="card2" accent="top" pad="25px 20px 0 25px" size="11.5" after="6" class="obstacles">

### What got in the way

- 500 N gauge limit: the strongest models couldn't be broken
- Hand-cranked loading, so the rate varied between trials
- Testing software deleted several finished datasets
- Identical prints gave different resistance readings

</Box>

<Box x="5.15" y="1.8" w="4.1" h="2.75" fill="red" pad="25px 20px 0 25px" size="11.5" after="6" bullet="white">

### What's next

- Try other materials: PETG and ABS
- Motorized, automated loading for consistent tests
- Aim: next-generation body armor, sports safety gear and medical prosthetics

</Box>
