The VBR Research Camp

Real engineering research, from first experiment to final paper

High school students work in small research teams on real engineering problems, exploring materials science, 3D printing and bio-inspired design, with daily instruction from Dr. Yang Yang of the SDSU Yang Lab. Each team carries its project from first experiment to a written paper and a final presentation.

How it works

A Typical Day

Every day runs the same research cycle: learn, experiment, check in, review.

Morning

Lecture

About an hour with Professor Yang on the day's scientific concepts, research methods and experimental techniques.

Midday

Team research

Teams run experiments with their teaching assistants, collect and analyze data, record observations and update their slides.

All day

Check-ins

Professor Yang visits every group to monitor progress, answer questions and help work through research problems.

Around 4 PM

Daily review

Each team and its TA meet with Professor Yang to discuss results, refine slides and prepare for the final presentation.

Performing experiments Recording data Analyzing results Organizing findings Preparing presentations

TAs assign each team member a different responsibility and rotate the roles through the program, so every student works through each stage of the research process.

Week by Week

Highlights from the camp newsletters.

Week 1 · Newsletter No. 01

Settling into the research cycle

Teams began the daily cycle of lecture, experiments, check-ins and afternoon reviews, with a team-building lunch to round out the week.

Equipment of the week

FLIR E8 Pro handheld thermal camera, which turns infrared radiation into thermal images so temperature differences can be found without touching anything. Infrared thermography is one of the bulky inspection methods the armor project hopes to make unnecessary in the field.

Week 2 · Newsletter No. 02

Distinguished guest seminars

  • Prof. Kuang, UW–Madison: “Adaptive Materials Enabled Additive Manufacturing”
  • Prof. Larson, Stanford: “Multimaterial 3D Printing of Living & Multifunctional Matter”
  • Dr. Zeng, USC: “Ultrasound Transducer Design”

Equipment of the week

Bambu Lab H2C multi-material, multi-tool 3D printer, one of the two printers used to make this project's CB-PLA and CB-TPU models.

Aerospace challenge

Coach Kelsey, an aerospace engineer and drone-competition coach, introduced students to drone competitions. VBR is forming a Drone Competition Team this fall.

Week 3 · Newsletter No. 03

Final presentations and a look ahead

Guest speaker

Chris Zheng, a rising high school senior and former VBR Summer Research Program participant, presented “Connect Your Research with the Real World”: advice on writing scientific papers, the publication process, presenting at academic conferences, and turning research into real-world impact through innovation and entrepreneurship. Hearing from someone who was in the campers' seats just a year earlier made the road ahead feel achievable.

Final Presentation Day

At the end of the three weeks of in-person labs, every team presented its research journey to parents and a panel of judges, from the research question and engineering design through testing, iteration, final design decisions and data analysis. The highlight was the Q&A, where judges and parents asked in-depth questions and the campers answered with confidence and clarity.

Mechanical Engineering TeamExcellence in Engineering Design Award

What's next

With three weeks of in-person lab research done, the teams moved on to six weeks of writing up their research papers.

The result

From Camp to Paper

The Mechanical Engineering Team's project, Bio-Inspired Multifunctional 3D Printed Armor, designed, printed and crush-tested lattices modeled on the deep-sea glass sponge and the mantis shrimp over four rounds of experiments, then wrote the results up as a research paper.
